“Power Requires Memory”


“Strength is what protects life.
Weakness is what harms it.
And we must choose strength.”


Those who believe themselves powerful are, in the long run, always revealed as the weakest. We do not remember the murderer’s rage, the thief’s greed, or the fool’s pride. These things burn fast and vanish. They leave no legacy except warnings.

If humanity is to move forward, it must overcome such illusions.
True power is found only in what endures.

It lives in the ancient love of a parent for their child — a force millions of years old, older than language, older than nations, older than every story we tell. When that love disappears, humanity disappears with it.

It lives in the awe the universe inspires in us, the quiet pull that drives our desire for knowledge and understanding.
It lives in the humility that lets us grasp that understanding — the recognition that learning is the point, the only point, of all this.

It lives in the belief that life is sacred.

These are the traits that will survive in any future humanity that does not collapse into pointless chaos. They are the load-bearing virtues of our species. They endure because they create memory — and memory is the only soil in which real power grows.

The shallow illusions of worth that thugs and strongmen revel in are less than yesterday’s moonlight, remembered only as examples of what we must avoid. Less than a forgotten footnote.

Greed, hate, rage, selfishness, pride, conceit, arrogance, disdain — these are weaknesses. Across millennia, humanity has tried to erase them, and when they do not fade, they self-destruct. They cannot build anything that lasts.

Power is not the theft of coals from another’s fire.
Power is warmth passed from one hand to another in winter.

That is what keeps the fire of humanity alive.
