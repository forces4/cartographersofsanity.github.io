What is known (neuroscience & psychology)
Panic temporarily degrades higher reasoning.
When threat is perceived, the brain shifts resources away from the prefrontal cortex (planning, logic) toward faster threat circuits.
→ This is not a character flaw. It’s biology.

You cannot “think your way out” of acute panic directly.
Trying to reason while the alarm is blaring often fails, because the system needed to reason is partially offline.

Physiological state precedes cognitive state under stress.
Breathing, posture, muscle tension, and sensory input affect cognition before thought does.

These three points are solid.

What is inferred (but well-supported)
Logic under pressure is not about being smarter.
It’s about keeping the thinking machinery online long enough to act deliberately.

“Emotional overwhelm” is often time compression + uncertainty + imagined futures hitting at once.

The mind opens the door to panic when it believes:

“I must act now, with incomplete information, and failure will be catastrophic.”

A usable model: Holding the Door
Think of panic as a battering ram, not a monster.
You don’t defeat it. You delay it.

Delay is victory.

Step 0 — The Prime Directive (memorize this)
Do not solve the problem while panicking.
First, reduce panic. Then solve.

If you remember only one thing, make it that.

The Door-Bracing Protocol (60–120 seconds)
1. Name the state (out loud if possible)
“I am panicking.”
“My system is overloaded.”

Why this works:
Labeling activates language circuits that partially counter the threat response.

This is not self-talk fluff. It’s neurological rerouting.

2. Buy time with the body (non-negotiable)
Choose one:

Exhale longer than you inhale (e.g., 4 in / 6 out)

Press your feet into the floor and notice pressure

Put a hand on something solid and cold

Assumption:
You have at least 10 seconds of physical control.
If you don’t, this becomes a safety situation, not a logic problem.

3. Ask ONE stabilizing question (not many)
Pick one and memorize it:

“What is the next safe action—not the final solution?”

“What would make this 10% less bad right now?”

“What am I assuming that I do not actually know?”

Do not ask “why” questions.
Why spirals. What stabilizes.

4. Constrain the problem
Say:

“For the next 5 minutes, the only goal is X.”

Examples:

Stay physically safe

Avoid saying something irreversible

Gather one missing fact

Logic fails when problems are infinite.
It works when problems are bounded.

5. Defer meaning
Tell yourself:

“Interpretation comes later.”

This is critical.

Most panic is not about the event—it’s about what the event means about:

your worth

the future

whether you are broken

Meaning-making is expensive. Postpone it.

How you “beat the beast”
You don’t overpower it.
You starve it of speed.

Panic wins by urgency.
Logic wins by sequence.

Even a bad plan executed calmly beats a brilliant plan executed in panic.

A hard truth (said gently)
You will not eliminate panic entirely.
Anyone who promises that is lying or selling something.

What is achievable:

Panic that is recognized sooner

Panic that lasts minutes instead of hours

Panic that no longer controls your actions

That is not weakness.
That is mastery.

One sentence to keep on the door
“I do not need clarity to act—only enough calm to choose the next step.”
