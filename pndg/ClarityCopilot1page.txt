THE ROB ONE‑PAGE LOGIC SHEET
A compact reasoning scaffold for tired days and sharp days alike.

1. The Four Moves
These are your compass points.

Want the Goal  
What am I actually trying to understand or decide?

See the Whole  
What’s the context? What’s upstream? What’s downstream?

Check the Path  
Does the reasoning chain actually connect?

Judge the Math  
Does the conclusion follow? Any contradictions?

2. The Five Operators
Use words or symbols — whichever feels natural.

AND (∧) — both must be true

OR (∨) — at least one is true

NOT (¬) — flips truth

IF…THEN (→) — one thing implies another

IFF (↔) — they rise and fall together

These five cover almost everything.

3. The Three Golden Rules
These are the “sanity anchors.”

No Contradictions  
If A and ¬A both appear, something’s wrong.

No Free Assumptions  
Every claim needs a reason.

No Unnecessary Baggage  
Prefer explanations with fewer invented parts.

4. The Tired‑Brain Method
When you’re foggy, use this version.

Name the claim (one sentence)

Ask what would make it false

Look for contradictions

Strip assumptions

Rebuild the simplest version that still fits the evidence

This is slow but extremely reliable.

5. Micro Set‑Theory Kit
Only the parts you’ll actually use.

is in → “X is a member of Y”

is a kind of → “X is a subset of Y”

A or B → union

A and B → intersection

nothing fits → empty set

Good for categories, definitions, and sorting ideas.

6. Emotional Logic Layer
Because feelings are part of the data.

Feelings = signals, not conclusions

If feeling contradicts belief → investigate

If belief requires ignoring your own experience → suspect

This keeps the system humane and grounded.

7. The Quark Model of Learning
Your metaphor was perfect.

You stretch

You snap back

But the center shifts

Repeat

Over months, the ground state moves

This is slow learning — but it’s deep learning.

8. Universal Application Rule
This is the “apply to anything” part.

Every question reduces to:

What is the claim?

What supports it?

What contradicts it?

What assumptions are hiding?

What’s the simplest version that still fits the evidence?

This works for beliefs, fears, arguments, decisions, memories — everything.
