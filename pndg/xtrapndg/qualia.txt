# Qualia as a “Keyhole” for Actualization: An Interdisciplinary Assessment of a Modal–Causal Hypothesis

## Executive summary

This report analyzes a specific hypothesis:

**Hypothesis (H):** *Qualia (subjective experience) are not merely correlates of physical processes, but the mechanism by which an “uncaused cause” (a metaphysical ground) turns an open “possibility space” into actual events—i.e., qualia occupy a “space between moments” and enable (or bias) transitions along certain possibility-paths.*

A useful way to study H is to separate at least three distinct questions that often get conflated:

1) **Metaphysical question:** Is there a coherent notion of an “uncaused cause” (or ground) that can “actualize” possibilities without presupposing prior causes? (Classic discussions arise in cosmological-argument traditions and in debates about necessity, contingency, and causal principles.) citeturn5search0turn5search12

2) **Mind–matter question:** What is the ontological status of qualia (reducible/irreducible; fundamental/emergent), and can qualia have **causal** efficacy given typical assumptions like “causal closure of the physical”? citeturn4search1turn4search13turn3search1

3) **Formal/physical question:** What does “possibility space → actuality” mean in formal terms (state spaces, branching-time semantics, probabilistic transition kernels, quantum state update, etc.), and is there *any* well-defined place in physics where “experience” could enter as an ontological selector rather than as a description of an observer’s information state? citeturn8search0turn1search2turn1search5turn11search2

Across philosophy of mind and cognitive neuroscience, “hard problem” and knowledge-argument literature treats qualia as difficult to reduce to functional/neural explanation, but that by itself does **not** entail that qualia are a mechanism of physical actualization. citeturn0search0turn0search5turn3search6

Across physics, the most mainstream accounts of the quantum-to-classical transition emphasize decoherence and environment-induced selection of robust “pointer states,” without requiring consciousness. citeturn8search0turn8search4 Nevertheless, some minority proposals have explicitly invoked consciousness in measurement (historically) or have proposed consciousness-linked objective-collapse models (more recently), but these remain speculative and face serious empirical and conceptual constraints. citeturn9search8turn1search15turn10search3turn10search5

**Coherence verdict (core idea):** H can be made *conceptually legible* only if it is sharpened into one of a small number of precise schemas (e.g., psychophysical laws that bias a transition kernel; a collapse model whose parameters depend on a mathematically defined “phenomenal structure”). Without such sharpening, H tends to commit category errors: blending **modal** talk (possible worlds / possibilities) with **causal** talk (production/difference-making) and blending **phenomenological** description (“between moments”) with **dynamical** description (state evolution). citeturn7search0turn11search2turn3search1

**Plausibility (as stated):** **Low.** The main reasons are (i) conflict with widely used physicalist closure constraints unless new psychophysical laws are posited; (ii) lack of a clear, testable mechanism; and (iii) no compelling empirical signal that “experience” is a distinct causal variable separate from neural/physical dynamics. citeturn4search1turn10search3turn2search1turn6search3

**Plausibility (weaker, reinterpreted variant):** **Medium** if “qualia as keyhole” is re-read *epistemically* (qualia as the **perspectival interface** by which an agent *represents* a world that is already being physically actualized), rather than *ontologically* (qualia as a fundamental selector that makes physical history). This weaker claim aligns more naturally with mainstream cognitive models and with interpretations of quantum theory that treat “measurement update” as an update in an agent’s expectations rather than a mind-driven physical collapse. citeturn2search0turn1search5turn1search13

## Definitions and conceptual scaffolding

This section gives **operational definitions** and highlights **ambiguities** that matter for H.

### Qualia

**Working definition:** qualia are the “introspectively accessible, phenomenal aspects” of mental life—what it *feels like* to see red, feel pain, etc. citeturn3search14turn3search6

**Key ambiguity relevant to H:**

- **Broad sense:** qualia = any phenomenal character (the “what-it-is-like”). This sense is hard to deny but is neutral about metaphysical status. citeturn3search14turn0search0  
- **Narrow sense:** qualia = intrinsic, non-representational “raw feels.” This narrow sense is more controversial and often at the center of anti-physicalist arguments. citeturn3search6turn0search5

H requires qualia to be more than “a description of experiential character”; it requires qualia to be a **causally relevant variable** or **ontological hinge**.

### Uncaused cause

In philosophically standard usage, “uncaused cause” belongs to families of arguments and positions that posit a first cause or a necessary ground (often within cosmological-argument traditions). citeturn5search0turn5search12

Two importantly different notions:

- **Temporal first cause:** a cause at the beginning of a causal chain in time. (This can conflict with cosmologies where time begins with the universe.) citeturn5search0  
- **Sustaining ground / necessary being:** not merely an earlier event, but an explanatory ground that “holds” contingent reality. SEP discussions stress that identifying the conclusion with God is not strictly part of the argument’s bare form, highlighting that “first cause” talk can be separated from specific theology. citeturn5search4turn5search8

H often tacitly assumes an “uncaused cause” that is active “between moments,” which is closer to a **processual or sustaining** notion than to a single temporal initial event. That assumption should be made explicit.

### Possibility space

“Possibility space” can mean several things; H depends on which one is intended:

- **Epistemic possibility space:** a set of states consistent with an agent’s information, often represented as a probability distribution over hypotheses.  
- **Physical state space:** the set of all physically allowed microstates/macro-states (e.g., phase space; configuration space). citeturn7search2turn11search0turn11search7  
- **Modal/metaphysical possibility space:** possible worlds and their structures, modeled via possible-world semantics and accessibility relations (Kripke frames). citeturn4search3turn7search1turn7search5  
- **Branching-time possibility:** a structure where the future branches into multiple possible continuations; often used both as (i) a formal semantics and (ii) a metaphysical conception. citeturn11search2turn11search5

H typically needs **ontic** possibility (not merely epistemic uncertainty): there must be multiple real candidate continuations *prior* to actualization.

### Actuality

“Actuality” can be:

- **Indexical actuality:** “the actual world” relative to a speaker/agent in modal semantics. citeturn4search3turn7search5  
- **Dynamical actuality:** the realized state of a physical system at time \(t\) (a point/region in state space). citeturn7search2turn11search7

H mixes these: it sounds like (i) “one branch becomes the actual world” and (ii) “one successor state becomes the next state.”

### Causation

At minimum, two major conceptions matter:

- **Difference-making (counterfactual) causation:** \(c\) causes \(e\) if, roughly, had \(c\) not occurred, \(e\) would not have occurred (an analysis associated with possible-world semantics). citeturn7search0turn7search4  
- **Productive/necessitating causation:** a stronger “making happen” relation (often invoked in metaphysical first-cause discussions, and criticized in some cosmological-argument debates). citeturn5search12

H reads like it wants productive causation (“brings possibilities into actuality”), but often relies on a difference-making picture (“biases which path occurs”).

### Emergence

A standard distinction:

- **Weak emergence:** higher-level phenomena are novel in practice but (in principle) derivable from lower-level dynamics.  
- **Strong emergence:** genuinely new properties/powers not reducible to lower-level facts, often requiring new laws and often conflicting with physical causal closure. citeturn3search1turn3search5

If qualia are a mechanism of actualization, H tends toward a strong-emergence or fundamental-property picture, unless it claims qualia are identical to specific physical dynamics (which then undermines the “keyhole” role).

## Cognitive science and neuroscience: accounts of qualia and the explanatory gap

### The “hard problem” and the knowledge-argument family

A major contemporary framing distinguishes “easy problems” (functional/behavioral capacities) from the “hard problem” (why and how there is subjective experience at all), emphasizing that mapping functions to brain processes doesn’t automatically yield an explanation of phenomenal character. citeturn0search0turn0search8

The knowledge argument (Mary-style) claims that complete physical knowledge might still omit knowledge of “what it’s like,” suggesting a gap between physical description and phenomenal knowledge. Modern surveys emphasize its centrality and controversy as an argument against physicalism. citeturn0search5turn3search6

**Relevance to H (inference):**  
These arguments support that qualia are explanatorily distinctive; they do *not* by themselves imply that qualia are a **mechanism of physical state transition**. H therefore needs additional premises: either (i) irreducibility plus causal efficacy, or (ii) identity between qualia and some selection principle in physics/dynamics.

### Mainstream neuroscientific theories and what they do (and don’t) explain

#### Global Neuronal Workspace

The global neuronal workspace (GNW) hypothesis models conscious access as “ignition” in a distributed, recurrent network enabling global availability/broadcast of information across specialized processors. citeturn2search1turn2search5turn2search9

**Constraint on H (known/inferred):**  
GNW is a functional-neurocomputational story: it targets *access* and reporting and correlates of conscious processing. It does not, as standardly formulated, posit a distinct “qualia field” or a causal selector external to neural dynamics. citeturn2search1turn2search5

#### Predictive processing / free energy principle

The free energy principle proposes a unifying picture of perception and action as prediction-error minimization/optimization in self-organizing systems. citeturn2search0turn2search8

**Constraint on H (inference):**  
This framework treats “possibility spaces” in a concrete way—as probabilistic generative models and posterior distributions over hidden causes. That makes “possibility space” precise but tends to interpret it epistemically (uncertainty about states), whereas H needs ontic branching or ontic indeterminacy. citeturn2search0turn2search8

#### Higher-order theories

Higher-order (HO) theories explain conscious mental states via higher-order representations (a state becomes conscious when represented in a suitable higher-order way). citeturn0search3turn0search15turn0search11

**Constraint on H (inference):**  
HO theories directly address why some information processing becomes conscious experience (in their terms) but generally work within a broadly physical/neurocomputational causal order. They do not naturally suggest that qualia are an extra mechanism for physical actualization. citeturn0search3turn0search15

#### Integrated Information Theory

IIT proposes that consciousness corresponds to a system’s capacity to integrate information, motivated by phenomenological properties like differentiation and unity. citeturn0search2turn0search6turn6search15

IIT has inspired operational metrics used in consciousness research, such as the perturbational complexity index (PCI), which perturbs cortex (e.g., TMS) and quantifies the complexity of distributed responses (TMS–EEG). citeturn6search3turn6search15

**Critical constraints (known):**  
Multiple critiques argue that IIT faces issues of unfalsifiability or formal non-uniqueness (e.g., the unfolding argument debate; concerns about Φ not being well-defined in some contexts). citeturn6search8turn6search0turn6search4  
There are also well-known “edge case” objections from theoretical computer science perspectives about allegedly counterintuitive consciousness attributions. citeturn6search1

**Relevance to H (speculative):**  
IIT is one of the rare contemporary frameworks that explicitly talks in terms of “intrinsic cause–effect power” and begins from phenomenological axioms. That makes it a candidate scaffold for formalizing a “phenomenal structure → physical selection” bridge—but it is precisely here that IIT’s disputed formalization and empirical status become decisive constraints. citeturn0search6turn6search4turn6search2

### Empirical “reality check” from the current state of consciousness science

A central tension in consciousness science is that multiple leading theories can accommodate broad patterns of data, motivating adversarial collaborations designed to pit contrasting predictions against each other. The Cogitate protocol explicitly frames GNW and IIT as leading competitors and aims at discriminating tests. citeturn6search10turn6search2

**Implication for H (inference):**  
Even within neuroscience, there is no consensus “mechanism of qualia” in the strong sense H requires. Theories focus on correlates, access, representational structure, integration, and reportability, with strenuous ongoing debate about how (or whether) these yield phenomenal character. citeturn6search2turn2search1turn0search0

## Physics and cosmology: causation, laws, and observer-related proposals

### Causation and “becoming” in physics

In many fundamental physical theories, micro-dynamics are time-symmetric, while macroscopic “arrows” (thermodynamic and causal intuitions) are often argued to emerge from boundary conditions and perspectival features. A well-known line of discussion in philosophy of physics emphasizes that intuitions about causation can be deeply tied to time-asymmetry assumptions. citeturn7search11turn7search19

**Relevance to H (inference):**  
If causation is partly emergent/perspectival, then “space between moments” may reflect a phenomenology of temporal perspective rather than a metaphysically real gap that needs a special mechanism. This is not a refutation of H, but it shifts the burden: H must show why the phenomenology indicates an ontic “gap” rather than an epistemic/perspectival structure.

### Quantum measurement and the possibility-to-actuality metaphor

Quantum theory is where “possibility → actuality” language most naturally appears (superpositions, measurement outcomes). But the mainstream story is nuanced:

- Decoherence theory explains how environmental interaction suppresses interference in certain bases and yields robust “pointer states,” giving an account of why classical-like behavior emerges, without assigning a special role to consciousness. citeturn8search0turn8search4  
- Interpretations diverge about whether there is a literal physical collapse, an ever-branching wavefunction, or a relational/information-relative state assignment. citeturn8search17turn1search2turn1search5

#### Three representative families

**Everett / Many-worlds:** Everett’s “relative state” formulation attempts to solve the measurement problem by eliminating collapse and explaining determinate records and probabilities within a branching structure. citeturn8search5turn8search17  
**Impact on H:** If all branches are real, “actualization” is not a single-world selection. H could be reinterpreted as *self-location*: qualia picks out *which branch is experienced* rather than which branch exists. That is a very different thesis from “qualia causes actualization.”

**Objective collapse models:** GRW-type models introduce stochastic, nonlinear modifications to Schrödinger evolution to ensure macroscopic definiteness. citeturn8search2turn1search7turn10search3  
**Impact on H:** Collapse theories are one of the few physically explicit “actualization mechanisms.” If qualia is to be an actualizer, it would most naturally appear as a parameter or trigger within a collapse dynamics—hence making H testable *if formalized*. But the default GRW/CSL family does not appeal to consciousness. citeturn1search7turn10search3

**Relational/information-agent views (RQM/QBism):** Relational quantum mechanics treats states as relational between systems, not observer-independent absolute states; QBism treats the quantum state as encoding an agent’s degrees of belief and uses the Born rule as a normative coherence condition. citeturn1search2turn1search5turn1search13  
**Impact on H:** These frameworks can make “update to actuality” a feature of information/relations rather than a consciousness-driven physical selection. H becomes more plausible only in a weakened epistemic or agent-relative sense.

### Observer-consciousness proposals: historical and contemporary

Historically, varieties of “consciousness enters measurement” ideas are associated with the von Neumann–Wigner lineage. A key reference is entity["people","Eugene Wigner","physicist"]’s 1961 “Remarks on the Mind-Body Question,” explicitly linking wavefunction description to “impressions” and consciousness in certain formulations. citeturn9search8turn9search0

More recent work includes explicit attempts to connect consciousness to collapse dynamics (e.g., a “consciousness-based quantum objective collapse model”). citeturn1search15

**Empirical and conceptual constraints:**

- Decoherence-based accounts and fully unitary approaches reduce the motivation for consciousness-triggered collapse, since much of the “classical appearance” can be explained via system–environment interaction. citeturn8search0turn8search4  
- Brain-based quantum coherence proposals (e.g., microtubule-centered theories) face strong decoherence-time objections and biological-feasibility critiques; Tegmark’s analysis argues relevant brain degrees of freedom are effectively classical (rapid decoherence). citeturn10search5turn10search1  
- Related critiques of proposed coherent condensates and exotic quantum mechanisms in biological contexts exist in peer-reviewed venues. citeturn10search2

### Cosmology, origin of laws, and “uncaused cause” analogues

“Uncaused cause” language in cosmology is often displaced into questions like “why these laws/parameters?” Several speculative programs attempt to make “law selection” tractable:

- entity["people","Lee Smolin","theoretical physicist"] proposes a cosmological natural selection mechanism (fecund universes) in which black-hole reproduction could vary effective parameters, giving an evolutionary-style explanation of apparent fine-tuning; he explicitly describes an observational falsification strategy in a later critical review. citeturn13search0turn13search8turn5search9  
- entity["people","John Archibald Wheeler","physicist"]’s “it from bit” and participatory-universe ideas connect physical reality to information-theoretic primitives and “yes–no” questions; Wheeler explicitly labels this a “participatory universe” in the cited text. citeturn13search10turn5search10  
- Humean and “best system” approaches to laws treat laws as descriptive summaries of patterns rather than governing necessities, potentially reframing “uncaused cause” talk as a question about global regularities rather than metaphysical production. citeturn5search7

**Relevance to H (inference):**  
These programs show that “law selection” can be approached without invoking qualia. For qualia to be *the* mechanism, H must specify why an experience-like variable is needed over and above physical/meta-law proposals.

## Formal models of “possibility space” and paths between states

This section attempts to make H precise by giving formal “slots” where a qualia-term could, in principle, be inserted.

### State spaces and dynamical rules

A dynamical system can be represented by a **state space** (or phase space) and an evolution rule mapping states to later states. citeturn7search2turn7search10

In that setting, a “possibility space” often means:

- the set of all admissible states \(X\), and  
- the set of admissible trajectories under the evolution map \(T\) (deterministic) or transition kernel \(P(x' \mid x)\) (stochastic).

### Modal logic and possible-world semantics

Modal semantics formalizes possibility by introducing a set \(W\) of worlds and an accessibility relation, with necessity/possibility operators evaluated relative to worlds. citeturn7search1turn7search5

### Branching time structures

Branching time (BT) is explicitly framed as (i) a family of formal structures, (ii) a semantics for temporal/modal logics, and (iii) a metaphysical conception about temporal modality. citeturn11search2turn11search5

BT provides a natural mathematical shape for “many possible futures, one experienced actual future”—but it does not, by itself, say what *selects* a branch.

### Stochastic processes and decision-structured transitions

A Markov decision process (MDP) models transitions from a state \(s_t\) to \(s_{t+1}\) given an action \(a_t\), with transition probabilities \(P_a(s, s')\). citeturn11search3turn11search12

This is a useful metaphor for H because it separates:

- **world dynamics** (transition kernels), from  
- **agent-related selection** (policies that choose actions).

If qualia were the “keyhole,” one interpretable move is: qualia instantiate or constrain the policy/action-selection function.

### Minimal toy formalisms for H

Here are three ways to “slot in” qualia. These are **not** endorsements; they are precision attempts.

#### Selection-function model on branching structures

Let a branching-time frame be \((W, \prec)\) with histories \(h\) through time instants. Define a **selection function** \(\sigma\) that picks one continuation at each branching point, yielding a single history \(h_\sigma\). citeturn11search2turn11search5

To represent H, one would posit:

- a mapping \(Q\) from physical states to qualia-states, and  
- a rule \(\sigma = F(Q(\text{state}_t))\), meaning qualia influences which continuation is realized.

**Testability problem:** Unless \(F\) implies deviations from known physical statistics, this becomes empirically inert.

#### Consciousness-modulated collapse-rate model (collapse-family embedding)

Start from a collapse model with parameters \(\lambda\) controlling collapse frequency/strength (as in GRW/CSL families). citeturn8search2turn1search7turn10search3

Then define:
\[
\lambda = \lambda_0 \cdot f(\Phi),
\]
where \(\Phi\) is a mathematically defined measure of “integrated information” (or other purported consciousness marker). citeturn0search6turn6search15turn6search4

This is similar in spirit to “consciousness-based collapse” ideas, but now it is explicit and, in principle, measurable via interferometry constraints. citeturn1search15turn10search3

**Hard constraint:** IIT’s \(\Phi\) itself is contested and may be non-unique in some formulations, complicating the model’s well-posedness. citeturn6search4turn6search8

#### Epistemic “keyhole” reinterpretation via agent-relative update

In QBist/pragmatist approaches, “collapse” is interpreted as an agent’s update of probabilities after experience; the quantum state is not a mind-independent physical field but a bookkeeping device for expectations. citeturn1search5turn1search13

Here, qualia are indeed the “keyhole” through which an agent *encounters* an outcome—but the mechanism is not ontological selection; it is principled belief update.

This makes H more coherent but changes its claim: qualia are interface-to-actuality, not the cause of actuality.

## Coherence analysis, objections, and research directions

### Where the hypothesis is strongest

H has a genuine intuitive target: **the felt “openness” of the next moment** and the sense that experience is not just “another object,” but is the field in which “now” and “this outcome” appear.

Philosophically, that intuition resonates with process-oriented metaphysics (reality as becoming rather than static being) and with views that treat experience as metaphysically fundamental or neutral-basic. citeturn4search2turn3search0turn3search3

Formally, H becomes sharpest when it is forced to specify:

- what “possibilities” are (epistemic vs ontic),  
- where branching/indeterminism lives (classical chaos ≠ ontic indeterminacy; quantum indeterminacy is candidate), and  
- what measurable difference qualia makes.

### High-probability category errors and failure modes

The following failure modes show up repeatedly when attempts like H remain informal.

1) **Modal–causal conflation:** “Possible worlds” in modal semantics are not causal competitors in the same spacetime; they are semantic/metaphysical constructs. Treating them like a physical branching process needs extra structure (a branching-time metaphysics, not just modal logic). citeturn7search5turn11search2turn4search19

2) **Epistemic–ontic slide:** predictive processing and many formal “possibility spaces” in cognitive science are about uncertainty over hidden causes, not about multiple real candidate futures “waiting” to be made actual. citeturn2search0turn11search12

3) **Experience–dynamics mismatch:** “space between moments” is a phenomenological description. Physics models state evolution with continuous or discrete dynamics; positing a literal ontological gap requires specifying what physical equations fail to cover and what replaces them. citeturn7search2turn8search0turn1search7

4) **Uncaused cause temporalization:** If an “uncaused cause” is outside time or is a sustaining ground rather than an event, then describing it as operating “between moments” may be incoherent unless one adopts a process metaphysics where the ground is immanent in becoming. citeturn5search0turn4search2turn4search6

### Major objections and best available replies

Here are the most central objections, stated in a form that directly tests H.

**Objection: causal closure of the physical blocks qualia-as-selector.**  
Physicalism’s causal-closure argument is often treated as a dominant motivation for physicalism; if every physical event has a sufficient physical cause, qualia cannot be an extra causal factor without overdetermination or violation of closure. citeturn4search1turn4search21

**Reply options (none cost-free):**  
Either reject closure, reinterpret closure (some argue formulations are flawed), or propose that the “qualia variable” is itself physical/neutral-base so it doesn’t add a new non-physical cause. citeturn4search17turn3search0turn3search12

**Objection: hard problem ≠ causal lever.**  
Even if explanatory reduction fails, it does not follow that qualia have distinct causal powers. citeturn0search0turn3search6

**Reply option:**  
Adopt a “psychophysical law” approach: treat correlations linking physical structure and experience as fundamental laws, potentially with causal roles. This is a substantive metaphysical addition, not a deduction from the hard problem alone.

**Objection: quantum-measurement appeal is usually a misunderstanding.**  
Standard decoherence accounts and many interpretations do not require consciousness; “observer” typically means a physical system, not a mind. citeturn8search0turn1search2turn1search13

**Reply option:**  
Embed H into a specific, testable collapse model where consciousness markers modulate collapse, thereby moving from metaphor to physics. But then it must confront collapse-model experimental bounds. citeturn10search3turn1search15

**Objection: biological feasibility of quantum mind mechanisms is doubtful.**  
Rapid decoherence arguments and critiques undermine microtubule-centered quantum-processing proposals as a basis for consciousness. citeturn10search5turn10search2

**Reply option:**  
Decouple H from microtubules: H does not logically require brain quantum coherence. It could instead claim that qualia are fundamental and only *correlate* with neural processes. But then the mechanism remains unspecified and risks unfalsifiability.

**Objection: modal realism deflates “actualization.”**  
If all possible worlds are equally real and isolated, “bringing possibilities into actuality” is not the right description. citeturn4search7turn4search19

**Reply option:**  
Adopt a branching-time metaphysics (not Lewisian modal realism) or reinterpret the keyhole as self-location/experience-selection rather than world-selection. citeturn11search2turn8search17

### Thought experiments and precision-building exercises

These are designed to expose where H is making a substantive claim versus redescribing known ideas.

**Zombie-branch experiment (conceptual):**  
Assume two physically identical branching histories (including identical reports), but one has qualia and the other is “phenomenal zombie.” If qualia are the selector of actual history, then the zombie branch should not be able to “instantiate actuality” in the same way—yet by hypothesis it is physically identical. This forces H either to (i) deny physical identity is possible, or (ii) claim qualia are identical to some physical structure after all.

**Mary-as-actualizer stress test:**  
Mary leaves the room and acquires new phenomenal knowledge. If qualia are an actualization mechanism, why would *knowledge acquisition* correspond to a *metaphysical selection power*? This probes whether H confuses epistemic access with ontic selection. citeturn3search6turn0search5

**Collapse-parameter embedding (toy model):**  
Define a clear consciousness proxy (e.g., PCI-like complexity or IIT-like Φ), then write a collapse-rate rule \(\lambda = \lambda_0 f(\text{proxy})\). The model is wrong if interferometry bounds or other collapse experiments exclude such dependence. citeturn6search3turn10search3turn6search4

### Research programs and experiments that could bear on H

The best prospects are those that force H into *differential predictions*.

#### Neuroscience program: dissociate “access/report” from “phenomenal structure”

The current adversarial testing culture (GNW vs IIT) is already moving toward sharper predictions. Extending that approach, one could attempt to identify signatures that correlate with phenomenology even when report/access is controlled, and then ask whether those signatures have any causal autonomy. citeturn6search2turn6search10turn2search1

A practical near-term strategy is to use perturbation-based indices (PCI-like) across conditions (sleep, anesthesia, disorders of consciousness) while examining which theoretical constructs actually generalize. citeturn6search3turn2search9

#### Physics program: test consciousness-modulated collapse vs standard collapse bounds

If H is to become physical and testable, it likely needs a collapse-family embedding. Collapse models have a substantial experimental and theoretical literature, including reviews of constraints and test strategies. citeturn10search3turn1search11turn1search7

The key empirical question becomes: does any measurable physical process behave differently in the presence of systems that plausibly instantiate consciousness proxies (high integration/complexity) versus not?

This is speculative, but it is the direction in which H can become empirically vulnerable.

#### Computational program: simulate “actualization by experience” in branching-time / MDP settings

A tractable computational research line is to implement:

- a branching-time environment (BT frame), citeturn11search2  
- an agent with an internal “experience state” \(q_t\), and  
- an explicit selection policy \(\pi(a_t \mid q_t)\) that influences transitions.

This does not prove H, but it clarifies what H would *mean* in an engineered system and what observational signatures would distinguish ontic from epistemic readings.

## Concluding assessment and prioritized sources

### Bottom-line plausibility rating

**Plausibility of H as stated (qualia as ontological mechanism of actualization by an uncaused cause): LOW.**

Reasons (with key uncertainties spelled out):

- **Mechanism deficit:** H lacks a specified mapping from phenomenal structure to state-transition dynamics. Without a transition law or parameterization, it is not yet a well-formed explanatory hypothesis.  
- **Pressure from closure and mental causation:** The standard causal-closure style argument strongly constrains any claim of non-physical causal influence; rejecting closure is possible but requires principled alternatives and typically some new empirical signature. citeturn4search1turn4search17  
- **Physics mismatch:** The physics arenas where “possibility → actuality” talk is natural (quantum measurement) have mainstream accounts that do not require consciousness (e.g., decoherence) and alternative interpretational frameworks that can treat “collapse” as relational/informational rather than consciousness-driven. citeturn8search0turn1search2turn1search13  
- **Empirical constraints:** Brain-quantum coherence proposals face serious feasibility objections; consciousness-linked collapse models remain speculative and would need to fit within tight experimental bounds on deviations from standard quantum predictions. citeturn10search5turn10search3turn1search15

**Key uncertainty that could raise plausibility:**  
A rigorous, predictive model in which phenomenal structure is mathematically defined and yields novel, testable deviations in physics (or yields unavoidable explanatory advantages in law-selection cosmology) could move H upward. No widely accepted model of this type currently exists in the peer-reviewed mainstream.

### A narrower, more plausible reinterpretation

If “qualia as keyhole” is made **epistemic** rather than ontic—qualia as the mode by which an agent *encounters* definite outcomes—then the idea becomes more coherent and aligns with (i) predictive processing’s emphasis on action-perception loops and (ii) agent-centered readings of quantum state update. citeturn2search0turn1search5turn1search13

This does not vindicate the “uncaused cause actualizes through qualia” claim, but it preserves the motivating intuition (“experience is the interface of definiteness”) without conflicting with standard physical constraints.
