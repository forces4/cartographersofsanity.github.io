The Universe, Becoming


The universe was never still.
Even in its first trembling breath
it leaned forward,
as if listening for the shape
it might one day learn to love.

Stars rose like questions.
Galaxies curled into themselves
like animals dreaming.
Matter rehearsed its lines,
trying on forms the way a child
tries on futures.

Life arrived not as an answer
but as a dare—
a whisper that said,
What if the cosmos could feel its own beauty
from the inside?

Across eons, it learned.
It learned to walk on continents,
to think in spirals,
to fear the dark,
to adore it,
to build meaning from the thin thread
between longing and possibility.

And still it grows.
Still it reaches for the version of itself
that would make even time pause
and say,
Yes. This is what I was for.

Perhaps one day
the universe will stand in its final form—
not perfect,
but pleased,
the way a tree is pleased
to have become a forest,
or a melody pleased
to have become a symphony.

And in that distant hour,
every star, every mind, every quiet atom
will know the truth it was born from:

The universe was never trying to be right.
It was trying to be beautiful enough
to recognize itself.
